## Intro

### Create UI designs that people love

When people love using an app or a website, it's often because the UI design hits all the right notes - it's easy to navigate, visually appealing, and intuitively laid out.

Getting to this sweet spot isn't accidental; it's the result of carefully applying UI design principles. These principles are the building blocks that not only create an aesthetically pleasing UI but also improve user engagement and satisfaction, encourage retention, and ultimately contribute to the product's success.

**UI and UX are two sides of the same coin.** You really can't have one without the other.

The magic happens when you get both right. You'll not only have a product that looks good but one that users find intuitive, user-friendly, and - most importantly - love to use.

## Visual Hierarchy

Visual hierarchy is all about how you arrange elements on a screen to show their level of importance. The aim is to guide the user's eye, attention and actions.

### Key Elements for Creating Hierarchy

- **Size** - Larger elements attract more attention
- **Color** - Darker and more vibrant colors draw focus
- **Weight** - Bolder fonts stand out
- **Position** - Elements at the top or center get noticed first

### Tips

#### Avoid prioritizing unimportant information

- Don't make product images too big, stealing spotlight from important details
- Don't highlight less critical information (like quantity over product name/price)

#### Prioritize important information

Before designing, list out features/elements and rank them based on importance to the user. Then use color, size, position, and contrast to effectively highlight key information.

#### Use color to communicate hierarchy

- Darker colors naturally draw more attention, signaling importance
- Lighter colors tend to recede into the background, indicating less significance

#### Use size and visual cues to differentiate information

By leveraging formatting options like font weight, font size, and color - as well as visual cues like icons - you can direct the user's attention to what truly matters.

## Proximity

Proximity is rooted in Gestalt psychology's "Law of Proximity": **elements close to each other are perceived as related, while those far apart are seen as separate.**

### Example

In a contact form:
- **Bad**: Same 28px distance between label and its field AND the field above
- **Good**: 12px from label to its field, 32px from label to unrelated field above

By correctly using proximity, you significantly reduce cognitive load, leading to a more efficient and enjoyable user experience.

## Clarity

When your interface is clear, users can easily understand how to interact with it without getting lost or frustrated.

### Three Common Mistakes

#### 1. Oversimplification

Going too far can backfire. Overly simplified design can lack essential context or information that helps users understand what to do next.

#### 2. Overwhelming Complexity

Interfaces packed with excessive options, buttons, and information overload the user, increasing cognitive load.

#### 3. Misguided UX

Even balanced designs can miss the mark by providing incorrect or misleading user experience.

## Alignment

Alignment refers to the arrangement of elements in a way that creates visual order and harmony.

### Types of Alignment

- **Left alignment** - Best for most text, creates a clean edge that's easy to read
- **Right alignment** - Great for numbers/prices (easier to compare values), and for RTL languages
- **Center alignment** - Perfect for titles or call-to-action buttons (avoid for longer text blocks)

### Tips

- **Use grids** - Implement a grid system for consistency across your UI
- **Use auto-layout** - Ensures every element is exactly where it should be
- **Be consistent** - Choose an alignment strategy and stick with it

## Contrast

Contrast helps make different elements stand out from each other.

### Why Contrast is Important

- **Readability** - Makes text and images easy to read
- **Guidance** - Directs user focus and encourages interaction
- **Accessibility** - Ensures content is visible to users with visual impairments (WCAG compliance)

### How to Apply Contrast

- **Color** - Use complementary or analogous colors
- **Font weight & style** - Utilize varying weights and styles
- **Size** - Use size contrast to differentiate elements
- **Borders & outlines** - Add necessary contrast when elements are similar in color
- **Shadows** - Make elements pop from the background

### Text Over Images

When designing text over images, ensure legibility at scale:
- Use an overlay with a layer blur
- Use a background blur

## Simplicity

Simplicity means reducing elements and content to their most essential forms.

### Why It's Important

A simple design reduces cognitive effort, making it easier for users to understand and navigate the interface.

### Understand What's Important

- Keep only the most important elements and information
- Every additional button, image, or line of text clutters the UI
- Don't show users information that isn't relevant to their task

## Whitespace

Whitespace (or "negative space") is the unmarked area between design elements.

### Benefits

- Gives your design room to breathe
- Enhances readability
- Guides the user's focus
- Helps break up a page

### Tips

#### Start with too much space

Having generous whitespace from the start gives flexibility to scale it down gradually.

#### Establish a spacing system

Stick with a base unit and work in multiples (e.g., multiples of 4: 4px, 8px, 16px, 24px, 32px).

#### Avoid random numbers

Numbers like 31px or 19px can throw off the harmony of your design.

## Layout

Layout is the framework that brings together aesthetics and usability.

### Good Layout Matters For

- **Aesthetics** - A visually pleasing layout catches the user's eye
- **Usability** - An intuitive layout guides the user logically through tasks

### Tips

#### Get the order right

Elements should be arranged logically: image first, then name, description, price, and finally the CTA.

#### Experiment with creative layouts

Traditional vertical lists can be bland. Reimagine options as selectable cards with labels, colors, icons, or images.

#### Form layout inspired by final output

Design forms to mirror how the submitted information will be displayed.

## Balance and Harmony

### Why It's Important

Balance and harmony help everything on your page fit together well, making the design look good and easier to use.

### How to Apply

#### Proportion and Scale

Make sure no single element overpowers the others.

#### Color Harmony

Avoid overusing a single color. Use complementary or analogous colors for variation.

#### Element Relationships

Maintain consistent spacing, typography, and element sizes.

## Consistency

Consistency helps create a unified and predictable user experience.

### Why It's Important

- **Ease of use** - Easier navigation
- **Builds trust** - Appears more professional and reliable
- **Reduces cognitive load** - Less mental effort required
- **Cohesive aesthetic** - Unified visual experience

### Key Areas for Consistency

#### Shape and Corner Radius

Maintain consistent corner radii for pictures and buttons.

#### Button Consistency

Don't use many different colors for primary, secondary, and tertiary buttons.

#### Image Consistency

Place images inside a consistent shape (like circles) and adjust size/positioning.

#### Card Length Consistency

Make all cards the same height. Align CTA buttons at the bottom if content varies.

#### Icon Consistency

Decide on an icon style (outlined vs filled) and stick with it. Exception: different states (e.g., filled icon for selected tab).

## Visual Cues

Visual cues help users understand your message faster and enhance overall user experience.

### Examples

- **Step indicators** - Use icons (pizza ordering, delivery truck, etc.) instead of just "Step 1, Step 2, Step 3"
- **Email inbox** - Use profile images/logos instead of just text for sender identification

## Depth and Texture

Depth and texture improve usability by creating visual hierarchies and making interactive elements more noticeable.

### Use Shadows

#### When to use

- Dropdown menus - Makes the menu stand out from the page
- Elements over dynamic backgrounds (maps) - Prevents elements from getting lost

#### Shadow Tips

- **Use soft shadows** - Harsh shadows look unprofessional
- **Match shadow color to background** - A grey shadow on purple background looks jarring; use a purple-tinted shadow
- **Define a shadow system** - Soft (buttons/cards), Medium (modals), Strong (dropdowns/alerts)

### Background Colors and Outlines

- Use a light grey background with white cards for a layered effect
- Use thin outlines to segment areas

#### Border Tips

- Don't overdo borders - can clutter the interface
- Borders should be thin and light
- Sometimes it's okay to break the rules (e.g., bold 10px border for a specific style)

### Glass Effect (Glassmorphism)

- Adds a modern, futuristic touch
- Use on contrasting backgrounds for readability
- Don't use on light backgrounds - compromises accessibility

## Color

Color sets the mood, conveys brand personality, and can influence user behavior.

### Less is Often More

Overusing primary colors can overwhelm users and detract from content. Use sparingly to highlight clickable elements.

### Background Color

- **Neutral backgrounds** (grey, beige, soft whites) let content and primary colors pop
- **Dark backgrounds** provide visually appealing contrast and reduce eye strain
- **Offer dark mode** - It's now expected by many users

### Don't Rely on Color Alone

Combine color with icons, text labels, or patterns for system states (errors, success, warnings).

### Stick to Color Conventions

- **Red** = Error/Stop
- **Green** = Success/Go
- **Yellow** = Warning

Don't deviate just for brand aesthetics.

## Typography

### Choosing the Right Font

- **Formal sectors** (law, accounting) - Serif typeface for classic, polished tone
- **Beauty/relaxed sectors** - Script typeface for warm, personalized feel
- **Tech/apps/messaging** - Sans-serif fonts for sleek, modern aesthetic

### Good Fonts to Use

Inter, Gotham, Lato, Open Sans, San Francisco, Helvetica, Manrope, Roboto

### Tips

#### Use max 2 different fonts

More than two fonts makes design look cluttered and off-brand.

#### Ideal line length

- **Desktop**: 45-75 characters per line
- **Mobile**: 30-40 characters per line

#### Break up long paragraphs

Shorter paragraphs offer visual breaks and natural stopping points.

#### Use subheadings

Subheadings make text easier to scan and highlight content.

### Text Contrast

- Avoid pure black (#000000) on white or pure white (#FFFFFF) on dark
- Use off-black or dark gray for headings, lighter gray for text

### Font Weights

- **Headings**: Bold, semibold, or medium
- **Paragraphs**: Regular or light

### Font Size

- Main text standard: 16px
- Minimum: 12px (below this is hard to read)

### Line Height

- Standard text (16px): 1.5 to 1.6 times text size (150%-160%)
- **Headings**: Smaller line-height (e.g., 130% for 36px heading)
- **Paragraphs**: Larger line-height

### Text Alignment

Left-aligned text is generally best for readability. Center or right alignment for special cases only.

### Things to Avoid

- Pure black/white for text
- Same color/weight for headings and paragraphs
- Bigger line-height for headings than paragraphs
- Bad contrast for aesthetic
- Font sizes smaller than 10px
- Extra thin font weights
- More than two different fonts

## Interaction Cost

Interaction cost is the mental, physical, and time effort a user invests to achieve their goal.

### Three Components

1. **Cognitive effort** - Mental processing power
2. **Physical effort** - Clicks, keystrokes, scrolling
3. **Time effort** - Duration to achieve goal

### Strategies to Minimize Interaction Cost

#### 1. Keep related actions close

Targets closer and larger are faster to click (Fitts's Law).

#### 2. Minimize choice

Fewer choices speed up decision-making (Hick's Law). Highlight recommended items.

#### 3. Use working memory efficiently

Organize information into meaningful, manageable chunks (~7 items).

#### 4. Reduce distractions

Remove animated banners, pop-ups, unnecessary visuals.

#### 5. Promote recognition over recall

Use common symbols, icons, logical layouts. Example: "Continue Watching" feature.

#### 6. Streamline tasks

Identify and remove unnecessary steps.

#### 7. Reduce repetition

Automate repetitive tasks.

### Practical Example: Product Page

**Bad**: Color selection via dropdown (2 clicks + scroll), quantity via dropdown, mouse movement to "Add to cart" = 5 clicks + scroll

**Better**:
- Show all color options visibly (1 click)
- Use +/- quantity selector
- Move "Add to cart" closer to quantity selector
- Add "Buy Now" for one-click ordering

## Bonus Tips

### 1. Reduce user barriers by exposing content directly

Don't hide content behind banners like "Discover 100+ recipes." Show the actual content immediately for:
- Quick value
- Reduced friction
- Better relevancy

### 2. Design fields for the type of input

- Verification codes: Separate boxes for each digit
- Card CVC: Short field (3-4 digits)
- Expiry date: Appropriate sized field

### 3. Don't use confirm password field

Instead:
- Use show/hide password toggle
- Use inline password validation with real-time feedback

### 4. Design better delete pop-ups

- Use red for destructive actions
- Include "Cancel" option as safety net
- Position "Cancel" on left, "Delete" on right

### 5. Avoid long blocks of text

Divide content into smaller sections with:
- Icons for key features
- Two-column layouts
- Comparisons and testimonials

### 6. Avoid distractions for task completion

Don't show irrelevant ads or content during checkout/sign-up.

### 7. You don't need to fill the whole screen

Concentrate fields in a defined box with ample white space around it.

### 8. Leverage overlapping images for visual depth

Use outlines matching the background to separate overlapping elements.

### 9. Design for the thumb zone

Place key elements (buttons, navigation, CTAs) within easy thumb reach on mobile devices.

### 10. Turn empty states into opportunities

Instead of "You have no projects," provide:
- Explanation of benefits
- Actionable tips
- Clear CTA

### 11. Turn mistakes into positive experiences

Good error states should:
- Clearly communicate what went wrong
- Offer actionable solutions
- Maintain consistent visual style
- Inject creativity and brand personality

### 12. Design better navigation dropdowns

- Include icons for visual interpretation
- Group related items under distinct categories
- Use images strategically (don't overwhelm)
